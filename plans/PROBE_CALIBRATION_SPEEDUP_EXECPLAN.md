## Purpose / Big Picture

The probe calibration measures where the centre of the WP-500 ruby ball sits relative to the robot flange by touching a fixed 25 mm calibration sphere 158 times under RSI (the KUKA RobotSensorInterface, which lets the PC stream position corrections every 4 ms). A full session takes about 16 minutes 16 seconds from the first to the last touch, and its accuracy figure χ95 (the 95 % quantile of the absolute verification residuals, about 0.11 mm) is 7.6 to 14.5 times larger than the touch repeatability, so most of the error is systematic and not yet explained. The effective ball radius that part measurement will use, r_eff = ρ − 12.5 mm (ρ: the fitted contact distance, 12.5 mm: the nominal sphere radius), cannot be checked by the sphere at all, because the sphere's own radius is not certified.

After this plan the user can: read, from the existing session records, which direction-dependent residuals recur across the recorded robot poses and which plan reductions would cost no accuracy (Milestone 1); add a 22 mm gauge block to the calibration session and get the effective ball radius from the block’s reference length, with its calibration uncertainty stated when available (Milestone 2); run the session with transfer moves at 15 mm/s and 100 mm/s² instead of 10 mm/s and 20 mm/s² (Milestone 3); and run every search with a smooth 2 mm pre-approach inside the RSI correction stream, so that only 1 mm is travelled at the slow search speed (Milestone 4). The expected time from the first to the last touch falls from about 976 s to about 528 s, plus about 3 minutes for the gauge block when it is included. Milestone 5 records, from the evidence of Milestones 1–4, whether a shorter calibration plan, a verification-only check before work, or a denser roll ring is justified; implementing those is left to a follow-up plan.

The proposals and estimates are in `docs/calibration/ROBOT_PROBE_SPHERE_CALIBRATION_SPEEDUP_RU.md`. The [GE-22 placement plan](END_GAUGE_WORKPIECE_PLACEMENT_EXECPLAN.md) supplies the placed gauge. Milestones 1 and 2 of the [planar probe scanning plan](PLANAR_PROBE_SCANNING_EXECPLAN.md) supply the fixed two-face points, ordered movements and common animation and sampling geometry. Calibration Milestone 2 integrates those inputs into one session and evaluates the detected positions. The operator-defined workpiece editor can be completed independently.

## Surprises & Discoveries

The raw single-cycle speed stayed within 0.95–1.05 mm/s for 25 consecutive intervals in just 5 of 1,264 searches. All 1,264 search-to-detection intervals had complete 4 ms IPOC spacing and finite joint samples. Absence of a plateau under this rule is a measured result.

- Observation: two full sessions are missing from the evidence the speedup document was first written from: `calibration_20261118_164135_671` and `calibration_20261120_135817_781`. The second has χ95 0.1086 mm, s_rep 0.0104 mm and 25 of 36 step-K7 sets outside their bound, values outside the earlier six-session ranges.
  Evidence: `settings/probe_calibration.json` (`lastCalibration`) and `plans/PROBE_PRIMARY_CENTRES_EXECPLAN.md`, Concrete Steps.
- Observation: the calibration directions sample only the cos 3ψ part of a three-lobed trigger-travel pattern and the verification directions only the sin 3ψ part (ψ: roll of the probe about its stylus). Calibration rolls 0°, 60°, …, 300° give 3ψ = 0°, 180°, …, so sin 3ψ = 0; verification rolls 30°, 90°, …, 330° give 3ψ = 90°, 270°, …, so cos 3ψ = 0. A sin 3ψ lobing therefore cannot be fitted from calibration touches but appears in the verification residuals with alternating sign. Milestone 1 tests this.
  Evidence: `CalibrationDirections` and `VerificationDirections` in `src/trajectories/probe/probetouchplan.cpp`.
- Observation: the job file of the controller program holds at most 600 steps (`DECL INT StepKind[600]` and the other arrays in `controller/krl/rsi/calibration/RSI_Probe_Calibration.dat`; `READ_JOB` rejects a count above 600). The current plan uses 485. A pre-approach as a separate KRL LIN per touch would add 158 steps (643), which is why Milestone 4 puts it into the correction stream; the reference gauge route of Milestone 2 adds 106 steps (591 total).
  Evidence: `RSI_Probe_Calibration.dat`, `RSI_Probe_Calibration.src` lines 130–131, `StepLimit = 600` in `src/trajectories/probe/probersistream.cpp`.
  For the reference route each four-touch gauge pass adds eight search/retract steps and three within-face transfers. Eight passes add 88 steps. Six changes of face, fixture or orientation add three steps each. Thus 485 + 88 + 18 = 591, leaving nine slots. Additional waypoints consume slots. Count the actual combined route and reject any export over 600 steps.
- Observation: the KUKA manuals in the manuals folder document jerk limits only for spline motions (`$GEAR_JERK`, `$JERK` for SLIN/SCIRC, "KUKA System Variables 8.1, 8.2 and 8.3", sections 3.66 and 3.87). How conventional LIN and CIRC shape the acceleration edges is not documented; the transfer times in this plan use the application's trapezoidal model and are estimates.

## Context and Orientation

The repository is a Windows Qt Quick application in C++17 (Qt ≥ 6.8, CMake, Eigen). Paths below are relative to the repository root, `D:\Qt\QtProjects\RoboCrap` or `E:\Qt\QtProjects\RoboCrap` depending on the computer; offline analysis works from a local copy of the session folders under `records/`.

Terms used throughout. A touch is one approach of the probe to a surface until the probe's trigger signal stops the robot. The probe WP-500 has a 30 mm stylus with a ruby ball of 3 mm diameter (ball radius r_b = 1.5 mm). A search is the slow constant-speed part of a touch (search speed v_s = 1 mm/s) during which the controller's RSI STOP object halts the motion at the rising edge of the probe input `$IN[8]`. A correction stream is the list of per-cycle position increments (one per 4 ms RSI cycle, X/Y/Z in WORLD, mm) that the PC sends during one search. The start shell is the sphere of radius 17 mm around the calibration sphere centre (contact distance L = 14 mm plus a 3 mm gap) on which the probe moves between touches with CIRC arcs. The waiting point is where a pre-approach hands over to the search. G1, G2, G3 are the three fixed probe orientations (KUKA A, B, C = 180, 0, 180: stylus down; 0, 90, 0: stylus along +X; 0, 0, 180: stylus down turned 180° about it). τ (tilt) is the angle between the stylus and the approach direction, ψ (roll) the rotation of the probe frame about the stylus; the approach direction in the probe frame {P} is (−sin τ cos ψ, sin τ sin ψ, cos τ). Class v holds G1 and G3 (stylus down), class h holds G2; each class has its own contact distance ρ_v, ρ_h. Step K7 of the method (`docs/calibration/ROBOT_PROBE_SPHERE_CALIBRATION_METHOD_RU.md`, section 6.3) groups the calibration residuals by group and tilt ring and by group and roll (36 sets) and flags sets whose mean exceeds 3·s_rep/√m (s_rep: repeatability from the twice-touched directions, m: touches in the set). χ95 is the 95 % quantile (value number ceil(0.95·m) of the sorted absolute values) of the verification residuals.

How a calibration session works today. In the application's Measuring mode, Calibration submode, Generate (`SceneCalibration::generate` in `src/application/scene/scenecalibration.cpp`) builds `ProbeTouchPlanParameters` (sphere centre, nominal radii, strut) and runs `createProbeTouchPlan` (`src/trajectories/probe/probetouchplan.cpp`) and `ProbeTouchMotion::create` (`src/trajectories/probe/probetouchmotion.cpp`) in a worker. The plan has 8 passes (calibration G1, G2, G3, G3, G2, G1 on 19 directions, the second three reversed; verification G1 on 25 and G2 on 19 directions), 158 touches. `probeMotionSteps` turns the plan into steps: per pass in a new group a LIN retreat of the previous pass, a PTP to the pass entry (60 mm out along the first normal and 80 mm up) and a LIN descend to the first start; per touch a CIRC transfer on the start shell (not for the first touch), an Approach (the search) and a LIN retract to the start. `TouchMotionBuilder` (same file) times every step with `MotionBuilder`; LIN and CIRC use the Generate transfer limits (`ChamferTimingParameters::transferFeed` and `transferAcceleration`, default 10 mm/s and 20 mm/s² in calibration Generate), the Approach runs at the constant search feed. `clearanceError` rejects any stored point whose ball is less than 3 mm from the strut, or less than 3 mm outside the contact shell except during Approach and Retract.

RSI Sampling pairs the saved motion phases with the KRL steps, prepares a 5 mm correction stream per search and exports `C:/RoboCrapJobs/calibration_data.txt`. It arms the recorder with the plan, job bytes and job ID. Start Calibration opens a session before requesting PLC program 1 in AUT EXT. During each search the application records the first valid detection for that touch number, including the six actual joint angles. After the run, `fitProbeCalibration` fits the ball centre, sphere centre and two contact distances from the calibration touches and computes χ95 from the verification touches.

The session writer stores `plan.csv`, `touches.csv` when detections exist, `residual_sets.csv` when the fit succeeds, the exported job for native sessions, the RSI and PLC tables when available, and finally `session.json`. A successful calibration stores its fitted values under `result`. Each CSV has one header and uses a semicolon separator. See `docs/code_context/RECORDS_FORMAT.md` for the columns and units.

The sphere file `resources/deploy/config/calibration_sphere.json` (deployed to `<build>/resources/json/calibration_sphere.json` and read there at every entry into the Calibration submode by `readSphereFile` in `scenecalibration.cpp`) holds version 1, `sphereDiameter`, `strutDiameter`, `strutRecess`, the `table` object (lengthX, widthY, sphereFromMinusX, sphereFromMinusY, sphereAboveTop) and `roughPoints` (X, Y, Z of the ruby ball centre at ten jogged touches, with the smartPAD's A, B, C, S, T). `SceneSphere` (`src/application/scene/scenegeometry.h`) carries these values; `sceneobject.cpp` draws the sphere, the table top (a `DrawingPlane`) and the stand as decorative parts. `settings/probe_calibration.json` has no `primary` object, so every recorded session was planned around the rough sphere centre from the jog touches.

Concrete Steps lists the eight reference folders selected in the speedup document. Each has `format` 1, `kind` `calibration`, `outcome` `ok`, 114 calibration touches and 44 verification touches. Their original identifiers are preserved in `session.json` under `converted_from`. A native session uses the same CSV schema. Select the eight named runs explicitly so additional sessions do not change the baseline or the 56 ordered comparisons.

## Idempotence and Recovery

The analysis scripts only read records and print; they can be rerun at will. Restore valid gauge observations and re-enter Calibration before generating a new job. Re-entering Calibration after a bad gauge reload cannot reactivate a prior combined job. Generate, Sampling and runs can be repeated; each successful Sampling assigns a job ID, and each Start Calibration opens a separate session. After any robot run that ends near the sphere or the block (a missed search, a `StepLimit` end), the user brings the robot back as the calibration contract requires: T1, jog clear, CELL BCO run to HOME, AUT EXT. If Milestone 4's stream turns out to misbehave on the controller, `searchStream` can be restored from git; the job format is unchanged in every milestone.
