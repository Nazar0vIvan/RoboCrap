My idea is the following. You can apply a rough definition of the coordinate system {W} to the end gauge using its three planes, and you can even then scan these three planes through the RSI to refine {W}. Thus, you can set the position of the end gauge on the scene and then scan the planes associated with 22 mm through the RSI.

I suggest simply creating three points (come up with them just for debug) for each of the three planes so that the end gauge deviates slightly at each of the three corners by literally several tens of mm, then writing the points to `\resources\deploy\config` in a json format like "end_gauge.json" similar to calibration_sphere.json . The 22x35x9 end gauge should be stand vertical, with the long side "along" Z_B, and X_B should rest against the 22x35 plane. The base coordinate system {B} coincides with the scene coordinate system.

With end_gauge.json, the end gauge should immediately load at the desired point with the desired orientation. Determining planes using three points, the intersection of these planes, etc.—that is, the entire calculation for determining the end gauge coordinate system {W}—is performed when loading it into the scene via `end_gauge.json`. Its {W} refinement via RSI can be saved in \settings and loaded afterwards just like for calibration sphere.
